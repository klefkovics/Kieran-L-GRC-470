Case Study 1 - Wild Rides Magazine

Project Goal

This assignment involved developing a magazine from the ground up. Starting from the conceptual stage, I was tasked with executing the branding, the magazine itself, as well as advertising and collateral.

Experience

From the outset, I aimed to make something unique. I developed a style and tone that stood out from traditional automotive publications and invited readers in. The challenging part was reigning in that personality to maintain consistent and professional branding.

Outcome

The end result was a project that I am very proud of. It expanded my skills as a designer and as an artist. If it weren’t fictional, I think Wild Rides would have real potential as a publication.

Case Study 2 - Chrysalis Funk Album

Project Goal

This assignment involved creating branding for a fictional band and producing packaging and promotional material for a tour of their latest album.

I took inspiration from the Funk movement of the 1970’s, the themes those musicians explored, and the social changes that were happening at the time, settling on an album about conformity versus rebellion during the Civil Rights movement. This assignment involved developing a magazine from the ground up. Starting from the conceptual stage, I was tasked with executing the branding, the magazine itself, as well as advertising and collateral.

Experience

Developing the identity of the band was challenging. However, after settling on a tone and a meaning behind the band’s work, the rest of the branding and illustrations fell into place much more easily.

Outcome

I am very satisfied with the outcome of this project. The butterfly motif and defiant, yet gentle tone of the imagery creates a strong throughline in the album’s branding and tells viewers what to expect from the music they’re about to listen to.

Case Study 3 - Graphic Novel Character Designs

Project Goal

My goal was to develop designs for the three major characters of my in-progress graphic novel, The Bluebird Inn. The designs needed to convey distinct personalities, social status, silhouettes, and symbolism, all while aligning with a specific setting: the 1950’s American ballroom scene.

Experience

After some experimentation early on, I settled on the main concept for each character that would remain through to their final design. I then created their outfits based on research into clothing from the time period. The process was continuously iterative. I made multiple sketches to get a feel for the characters’ shapes and gestures, making many small tweaks to the designs along the way.

Outcome

Developing these characters was a long process, but a necessary one to refine them to the best of my ability and to become comfortable enough drawing them to do so consistently. This project expanded by abilities as an artist and took me out of my comfort zone, and the result is three of my favorite characters I’ve ever come up with.
